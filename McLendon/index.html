01 AI as Software EngineerBuild a Halftime Trivia App

Complete CRAFT-I Prompt

Context

Our college football team wants to boost fan engagement at halftime. Fans scan a QR code on the stadium screen and play a quick trivia game on their phones. A local sponsor will “present” the game.

Role

Act as a senior app developer who builds fan engagement products for sports teams.

Action

Build a working halftime trivia game: 10 multiple-choice college football questions, a 15-second timer per question, a running score, a results screen with a shareable “My Score” message, and a “Presented by [Sponsor Name]” banner.

Format

A single-page web app that runs right here in the chat preview. Mobile-friendly, no login, no outside data.

Tone

High-energy and game-day bold, in our team colors: navy and gold.

Interview Me

Optional: Before building, ask me about our team, our audience and what the sponsor wants.

What would you change before sharing or using the result?

02 AI as TeammateThe Crisis

Complete CRAFT-I Prompt

Context

I work in communications for a Division I athletic department. At 9 p.m., a video surfaced of our star quarterback in a heated confrontation with a fan after Saturday’s loss. It has 200,000 views, reporters are calling, and our coach hasn’t commented. Our next game is in 4 days.

Role

Act as a seasoned sports crisis communications director with college and pro experience.

Action

Build my first-24-hours response plan: who to inform and in what order, a draft holding statement, what we should and shouldn’t say, 3 ways this could escalate and how we’d respond, and the most common mistake teams make here.

Format

A header per section. Holding statement under 60 words. Escalation scenarios in a table.

Tone

Calm, decisive and professional, like a briefing to the athletic director.

Interview Me

Optional: Before you start, ask me up to 3 questions about facts you’d need.

What would you verify before trusting the response?

03 AI as Thought PartnerThe Business Decision

Complete CRAFT-I Prompt

Context

I’m on the business team of a minor league baseball team. Attendance fell 12% this season, mostly families. Option A: cut ticket prices 20%. Option B: keep prices and invest $250,000 in game-day upgrades (kids’ zone, themed nights, better food). We can only afford one.

Role

Act as a sports business strategist who advises teams on revenue and fan engagement.

Action

Compare both options on attendance, revenue, fan loyalty and brand. Name the assumptions each depends on and the data to check first. Recommend one with your reasoning, then argue against your own recommendation.

Format

One-paragraph recommendation, a side-by-side table, a bulleted data list, then the counter-argument.

Tone

Direct and analytical, like a memo to the team president.

Interview Me

Optional: Ask me 3 questions about our market before you analyze.

Which assumptions or missing data change your recommendation?

04 AI as Pressure TesterThe Deal

Complete CRAFT-I Prompt

Context

Our women’s college basketball program was offered a 3-year, $150,000-per-year deal by a fast-growing energy drink brand. They want courtside signage, player social posts and naming rights to our halftime show. Several athletes have NIL deals with competing beverage brands. Our fans skew toward families.

Role

Act as an experienced sports partnerships executive who has negotiated college and pro brand deals.

Action

Pressure-test this deal: rank the biggest risks (brand fit, athlete conflicts, compliance, fan reaction), list what to negotiate or push back on, the questions to answer before signing, and 3 talking points for my meeting.

Format

Ranked risks with a one-line “why it matters,” negotiation bullets, numbered talking points.

Tone

Confident and commercially savvy.

Interview Me

Optional: Ask me what outcome matters most to our program before you start.

What risks or negotiation questions still need a human decision?
